Why do I get black bars on my ultrawide monitor?

Most video is 16:9 (like 1920×1080). Your monitor is 21:9 (3440×1440) or wider. The video can't fill the extra width, so the player letterboxes it with black bars on the sides. Ultrawider fills those bars — Smoothie stretches without cropping, Crop zooms with cropping.

Why do faces look wide and fat when I stretch video?

Uniform stretching makes every pixel ~33% wider on a 21:9 screen. A face that's naturally oval becomes round. Smoothie fixes this with a non-linear warp: edges stretch far more than the center. The middle of the frame — where faces are — stays nearly unchanged. Read the full explanation →

Does Ultrawider work on Netflix?

Yes. Works on Netflix, Prime Video, Disney+, Twitch, Crunchyroll and other DRM-protected platforms. The warp engine applies after the DRM pipeline renders — no protection is bypassed.

What's the difference between Smoothie and Crop?

Crop: instant zoom, cuts top/bottom, works everywhere, free.
Smoothie: non-linear stretch, nothing lost, faces stay true, works on DRM. Premium mode.

Which browsers are supported?

Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera — any Chromium browser. Firefox not supported (rendering limitations).

Will Smoothie make the picture blurry?

On good sources (1080p+, decent bitrate) — no, it looks great. On low-quality sources (sub-720p, heavy compression), stretching amplifies artifacts. Ultrawider's quality indicator warns you and recommends Crop when the source is too soft.

How is this different from player settings?

Player "stretch" makes everything wide. Player "zoom" is just Crop. No player offers non-linear warping. Ultrawider adds both modes + quality recommendations + one-click switcher, automatically on every site.
