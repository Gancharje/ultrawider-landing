Black bars on ultrawide: three ways to kill them

Stretch, crop, or warp — which one is right for you?

Your ultrawide monitor is 21:9. Most video is 16:9. The mismatch creates black bars on the left and right — sometimes 25% of your screen is wasted. Here are the three approaches, honestly compared.

Option 1: Uniform Stretch

How: Change aspect ratio to "stretch" or "full" in your player.

Pros: Nothing is lost. Works everywhere.

Cons: Faces look 34% wider. Everything becomes bloated. Fine for landscapes, terrible for people.

Verdict: Better than bars, but the distortion is distracting.

Option 2: Crop / Zoom

How: Change aspect ratio to "zoom", "crop" or "fill". Or use Ultrawider's Crop mode.

Pros: No distortion — the picture looks natural. Instant, works on any content.

Cons: Loses the top and bottom of the frame — about 25% of the content. Subtitles at the very bottom can get cut (Ultrawider's Crop preserves the subtitle zone).

Verdict: Best for action scenes and scenery. Worst when subtitles or important action is at the top/bottom.

Option 3: Non-Linear Warp (Smoothie)

How: Ultrawider extension — Smoothie mode.

How it works: Instead of stretching every pixel equally, it stretches the EDGES far more than the CENTER. A face in the middle stays at 1.0× (zero distortion). A tree at the edge gets 1.86× (invisible because you're not looking there).

Pros: No black bars. No cropping. No face distortion. Works on Netflix and DRM.

Cons: Not available in any video player — requires the extension. On very low-quality sources, stretching can soften the picture.

Verdict: The best option when it works. Which is almost always.
