The math · One uniform

Quintic smootherstep. Drag and see.

Every horizontal pixel is remapped by blending the identity line with Ken Perlin's quintic smootherstep — chosen because its second derivative vanishes at the endpoints, so the warp accelerates without a visible "seam" between centre and edge. At the default strength of 0.34, the centre matches a 21:9 monitor's 1.31× horizontal stretch exactly: faces stay round.

0 · off (fat) 0.34 · natural 1.0 · over (thin)
f(x) = mix(x, 6x⁵ − 15x⁴ + 10x³, 0.34)

C² continuity established in Perlin, Improving Noise (SIGGRAPH 2002) · runs in ~3 multiply-adds per pixel — shipped as an SVG displacement filter that never reads a pixel, which is why it works even on DRM video (Netflix, Prime).

Free gives you the full Smoothie warp on YouTube — forever — plus a 3-minute Smoothie preview on any other site, as many times as you like: watch it fill your own Netflix or Twitch before paying anything. Which monitors are supported?

Any ultrawide: 21:9 (2560×1080, 3440×1440), 24:9 (3840×1600), 32:9 super-ultrawide (5120×1440), or anything wider than 16:9. The extension auto-detects your viewport aspect and tunes the curve depth so the warp stays in the sweet spot regardless of display.

Which sites does it work on?

Everywhere video plays in your browser — including Netflix, Prime Video, Disney+, Max, Hulu, Apple TV+ and Crunchyroll. Smoothie is built as an SVG displacement filter: it never reads a single pixel, so DRM has nothing to block. Sports too: DAZN, ESPN, F1 TV, live matches on any streaming site. Plus Twitch, Kick, Vimeo, news sites, course platforms — and every other HTML5 player.

We don't refund "I changed my mind" requests, but we won't hold anyone hostage to something that's actually broken either. See our Support Policy for the full picture.

Does it work in all browsers?

Chrome 110+ and any Chromium-based browser of the same vintage (Brave, Opera, Vivaldi, Arc) via the Chrome Web Store, Microsoft Edge via Edge Add-ons, and Firefox via addons.mozilla.org. Safari is not supported; the live playground on this page works in any modern browser with WebGL2.
